Where does "energy out" go?

🫀 Just running your body (heart, brain, breathing, growing)~60 to 70%
🏉 Moving: sport, walking, fidgeting~15 to 30%
🍲 Digesting food~10%

💡 Most of your energy is used just being alive. Teens are still growing, so needing a lot of fuel is completely normal, especially with sport.

kJ or kcal? 🔄

Labels in New Zealand and Australia show kilojoules (kJ); US labels show Calories (kcal); UK and EU labels show both. Most apps use kilocalories (kcal), usually just called "calories".

Per serve vs per 100 g: the 2-second rule ✌️

🥄

Per serving tells you what you actually eat, if you eat the serving size on the pack. Check it: a "serve" of chips might be way smaller than the bag!

⚖️

Per 100 g lets you compare two products fairly, like two cereals or two yoghurts, because the amount is the same. Lower sugar and sodium, higher fibre and protein per 100 g usually wins for everyday foods.

A typical day. Tap a stop for tips.

🏋️ Training early, before breakfast?

Some of us like to train first thing on an empty stomach ("fasted"). That's fine for lots of adults. A few tips to get the most from it:

  • 💧 Drink a glass or two of water when you wake up.
  • 🍳 Eat a proper breakfast within an hour or two after: protein plus carbs, e.g. eggs on grainy toast, or wheat biscuit cereal with milk and a banana, plus a coffee if you like.
  • 🍌 Long or very hard session? A small snack first (banana, slice of toast) can help performance.
  • 🥩 Aim for about 25 to 40 g protein after training and spread protein across the day.
  • 🧒 Under 18 and training early? Have a small snack first (banana, toast, a glass of milk); growing bodies need the fuel.

🌟 The big ideas

  • 🥚 Protein at every meal keeps you full and helps muscles recover.
  • 🍝 Carbs around activity: they're your main fuel for sport and brains.
  • 🍎 Everyday snacks: fruit, yoghurt, cheese and crackers, nuts, toast.
  • 🍫 Treats sometimes, ideally with a meal, enjoyed properly rather than grazed all evening.
  • 💧 Water first when you're thirsty.
  • 🌙 Light finish: try not to have a big meal right before bed.

🎒 Teen zone: school, after school and sport

🥪 School lunch ideas

Pick one from each line and you're sorted:

🍞 Base: wholemeal or grainy sandwich, wrap, leftover pasta or rice, sushi

🍗 Protein: chicken, tuna, egg, ham, cheese, hummus, leftover mince

🥕 Colour: carrot sticks, cherry tomatoes, cucumber, an apple, mandarin or kiwifruit

💧 Drink: water bottle (refill it all day)

Tip: make lunches the night before with dinner leftovers. Future you will thank you.

🍌 After-school snacks

After-school hunger is real, especially for growing teens. Go for these first:

  • 🧀 Cheese and crackers
  • 🫙 Yoghurt with fruit or muesli
  • 🍞 Toast with peanut butter, banana or a yeast spread
  • 🥛 A glass of milk or a malted milk drink
  • 🍎 Fruit plus a handful of nuts
  • 🍳 Eggs, or leftovers from last night

Having sport later? Make it a slightly bigger, carb-based snack.

🏆 Fuelling for sport

🍝3 to 4 h beforeA normal meal: carbs + protein + veg.
🍌1 to 2 h beforeSmall, easy carb snack: banana, toast and honey, muesli bar.
💧DuringSip water. Long tournament day? Add fruit or sandwiches between games.
🥛Within ~1 h afterProtein + carbs: flavoured or malted milk, yoghurt and fruit, or dinner.

🥤 Drinks: how many teaspoons of sugar?

Each cube = 1 teaspoon (about 4 g) of sugar. Approximate, per typical serve.

💡 Milk and juice sugars come naturally from the milk or fruit (milk also brings protein and calcium), but juice has lost the fibre of whole fruit, so a whole orange beats a glass of OJ. ⚡ Energy drinks also pack a lot of caffeine; their labels say they're not suitable for children, and they're a poor match for sport and sleep.
